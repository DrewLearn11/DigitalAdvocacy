Save Our Forests

Deforestation and jungle degradation in the Philippines is more than the loss of trees. It is the loss of the ecosystems that protect people, wildlife, and water.

Learn the Facts

1. What is the Issue?

Deforestation is the large-scale removal or loss of forests, often caused by human activities such as logging, agricultural expansion, land conversion, infrastructure development, and settlement. Forest degradation occurs when forests remain but lose part of their ecological quality, biodiversity, and ability to provide important ecosystem services.

The Philippines has experienced a major decline in forest cover over the past century. According to the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), forest cover declined from approximately 21 million hectares in the early 1900s to about 7.2 million hectares. Major factors include logging, agricultural expansion, and urban development.

2. Current Status

Deforestation and forest degradation remain important environmental concerns in the Philippines. Recent Global Forest Watch data shows that between 2001 and 2025, the Philippines lost approximately 1.6 million hectares of tree cover, equivalent to an 8% decrease from its 2000 tree-cover area. The estimated loss was associated with approximately 960 million metric tons of CO₂-equivalent emissions.

Palawan: The Last Ecological Frontier

Palawan is one of the provinces that cover the largest areas of Philippine forest, known as the country's "last ecological frontier" as it holds roughly one-tenth of the national total of forest land.

In 2020, Palawan had 1 megahectare (1.0 Mha) of natural forest, covering 69% of its land area. By 2025, it had lost 4.5 kilohectares (4.5 kha), or at least 0.45%, leaving a total of 0.9955 Mha. The lost forest is equivalent to the area of 1,234 Philippine Arenas, and is associated with 2.6 megatonnes (2.6 Mt) of carbon dioxide (CO₂) emissions.

Cleopatra's Needle Critical Habitat

In November 2016, the Palawan Council for Sustainable Development (PCSD) officially declared the Cleopatra's Needle Forest as a critical habitat. The declaration was made to protect the mountain's different species of flora and fauna.

According to the Global Wildlife Conservation, the mountain's 40,000-hectare forest is a safe haven for unique and threatened species, including the Philippine Cockatoo, the Palawan Forest Turtle, the Palawan Bearcat, the Palawan Horned Frog, the Palawan Toadlet, the Philippine Flat-headed Frog, and the Palawan Monitor Lizard.

3. Major Causes

The Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan identifies logging, agricultural conversion, kaingin, forest fires, and other disturbances among the direct causes of deforestation.

Logging

Removal of trees for timber and other forest products.

Agricultural Expansion

Forests are converted into agricultural land.

Land Conversion & Urban Development

Forests may be cleared for settlements, roads, and other infrastructure.

Kaingin

Slash-and-burn cultivation: vegetation is cleared and burned to prepare land for farming.

Fires & Disturbances

Fires and natural disturbances can damage forest ecosystems.

4. Effects on Humans and the Ecosystem

Deforestation affects both people and the natural environment.

Effects on Humans

  • Greater risk of flooding and landslides
  • Increased soil erosion
  • Reduced availability and quality of water
  • Loss of forest resources that support communities and livelihoods
  • Increased vulnerability to environmental and climate-related problems

Effects on Ecosystems

  • Loss and fragmentation of wildlife habitats
  • Decline in biodiversity
  • Disruption of natural ecological processes
  • Soil degradation and erosion
  • Reduced ability of forests to store carbon
  • Increased sedimentation that can affect rivers and coastal ecosystems

The Philippine Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan reports that deforestation can contribute to increased flooding and drought, erosion, landslides, siltation of coral reefs, and reduced groundwater supplies. It also emphasizes that forests provide important ecosystem services that support human well-being and environmental health.

5. What Does the Quantitative Data Tell Us?

The available data shows that the problem is significant rather than only theoretical.

More recently, Global Forest Watch recorded approximately 1.6 million hectares of tree-cover loss from 2001 to 2025. It also found that 75% of tree-cover loss occurring from 2021–2025 was within natural forests.

These figures show that human activities that change forest land can have a large cumulative effect. The loss of forests does not only mean fewer trees; it can also mean the loss of habitats, ecosystem services, carbon storage, and natural protection against environmental hazards.

6. Our Insight and Analysis

The information shows that deforestation is both an environmental and a human issue. Forests provide services that people often depend on without directly noticing them:

  • Water regulation
  • Soil protection
  • Carbon storage
  • Wildlife habitat

The quantitative data also shows that forest loss has occurred over a long period and continues to be monitored today. Protecting remaining forests and reducing activities that cause unnecessary forest loss are important for maintaining biodiversity, ecosystem stability, and human well-being.

Our conclusion: Deforestation and jungle degradation in the Philippines should be understood not simply as the loss of trees, but as the degradation of ecosystems that support people, wildlife, water resources, and the overall health of the environment.
